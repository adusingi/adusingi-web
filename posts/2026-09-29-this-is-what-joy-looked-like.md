3,258,964,255 tokens.

This is what joy looked like on my OpenAI dashboard.

Between 28 August and 16 September, I sent 25,421 requests. The bill was $2,320.79.

I am not sharing this because a large number is an achievement. Tokens are not work. A bill is not learning. Commits are not value.

I am sharing it because the dashboard cannot show the part that mattered: I was happy.

## What I Was Building

For almost three weeks, I kept returning to the work because I wanted to know what I could make next.

I turned Doko Maps into an itinerary workspace for travel professionals. I prepared new training material for the people I teach. I built a terminal version of Clarity Board. I learned Go and built Indebero, a small monitor for the services I run.

Across the same period, that work produced 287 commits without counting merges: 170 for Doko Maps, 46 for the Academy, 60 for Clarity Board, and 11 for Indebero.

## The Other Half Is Pleasure

Useful things came out of those weeks. But usefulness is only half of the story.

The other half is pleasure.

I love beginning with something I do not yet know how to build. I love watching an idea become something I can use. I love reaching the first working version and discovering that it has opened a better question. And I love turning what I learn into material another person can use.

I could not have spent this much time if I were bored.

I was not forcing myself through a long task. I was following my curiosity.

## Learning Was Part of the Pleasure

I was not playing around. I was learning. But learning was not a duty I had to endure before I could build something useful. Learning was part of the pleasure.

AI did not remove the work or the pleasure. It gave my curiosity somewhere to go. Each answer let me try another idea, make another choice, and see a little more of what I could build.

Was the code useful? Yes.

Was all that code also pleasure?

For me, absolutely.

This is how I enjoy working: learn, build, use what I built, then teach what I learned.
